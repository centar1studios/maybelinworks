Project 01

Educational Publication

Fenix

Fenix is a travel-focused publication created during my final semester of college, documenting kid-friendly destinations across Arizona through the perspective of traveling with my daughter.

Designed with other single mothers in mind, the publication combines personal storytelling with recommendations for families looking to explore Arizona together.

One featured spread uses an Old English-inspired typeface recreated entirely by hand using sprinkles from my pantry, individually placed with tweezers.

All design, typography, and photography were created by me.

Project 02

Educational Branding

Casa Guadalupe

Casa Guadalupe is a conceptual brand identity created to connect with parents between the ages of 25 and 35 and build a welcoming sense of community around education.

The final visual direction was inspired by the colors and patterns of a zarape blanket I discovered while visiting Downtown Los Angeles with my family shortly before the COVID-19 shutdown.

Growing up in Los Angeles, visits to Placita Olvera and yearly Mexican celebrations were an important part of my upbringing. The energy of the vendors, traditions, colors, and gathering spaces represented the sense of community I wanted to bring into Casa Guadalupe's identity.

The resulting branding uses vibrant color and pattern to create a visual language rooted in education, connection, warmth, and cultural memory.

Educational Branding

About Me

The artworks I share here take me back to a time when life was simpler, when I was just beginning to discover the curious, wandering young girl within me. Growing up in Los Angeles exposed me to so many trends, cultures, and experiences. Thanks to my parents, I grew up visiting museums, sunset beaches, amusement parks, taquerias, public parks, and spaces filled with public art.

My work mirrors who I was, who I am, and who I want to become. This often translates into bold, unapologetic work that uses my artistic voice to explore difficult conversations around identity and bias.I am a woman first, then an artist. Femininity plays an important role in my visual language, especially within my typography, which I carefully nurture to communicate emotion, personality, and intention.

As a mom to an 18-year-old, I also deeply value education. My lived experiences as a woman, mother, and artist continually find their way into the work I create.I never gave myself the title of multidisciplinary artist, but as I continue to evolve internally, I have become more comfortable embracing that identity externally and sharing everything it allows me to create.
